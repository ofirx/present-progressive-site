The Split

Descend through the haunted house

Entrance Hall

Begin inside the mind

Visitors begin at the entrance hall, where the two stories and the central idea are introduced: in both texts, the apparent threat seems external, but the deeper source of horror lies within the narrator’s own mind.

Upper Floors

The surface of the stories

The upper floors present the visible elements of the stories, including brief summaries, key quotations, the narrators’ crimes, and Poe’s use of mirrors, repetition, sound, masks, enclosed spaces, and unreliable narration. These rooms are designed to feel relatively orderly, representing the surface level of the narratives.

Lower Levels

History, culture, and now

As the visitor moves lower through the house, the focus shifts towards historical and cultural interpretation. These windows explore Poe’s nineteenth-century American context, including individualism, social respectability, crime, urban growth, sensational print culture, and anxieties beneath national ideas of progress and rationality. The next level connects the stories to contemporary American life through issues such as curated online identities, narcissism, social pressure, anxiety, true-crime culture, and the need to appear controlled.

Basement

The deepest interpretation

The basement contains the project’s deepest interpretation. Freud’s ideas about the id, ego, superego, the uncanny, repression, and the fear of eyes provide a later psychoanalytic lens for reading Poe. The descent ends with the argument that guilt and conscience cannot simply be silenced or buried. In both stories, repression returns through reflection, sound, repetition, and confession.
