Sentence structure

A strong sentence has a subject (who or what) and a predicate (what happens).

Subject + predicate

The subject performs the action or is described. The predicate tells what the subject does or is.

The river (subject) flooded the road (predicate).

Fragments

A fragment is an incomplete thought. It cannot stand alone as a sentence.

Because the lights went out.

Because the lights went out, we lit candles.

Run-on sentences

Two complete thoughts jammed together without proper punctuation create a run-on.

I finished my homework I watched a show.

I finished my homework, and I watched a show.

I finished my homework. I watched a show.
